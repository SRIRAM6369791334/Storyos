# ADR-0004: Canon Modification Requires Explicit Human Confirmation

> **Status:** Accepted
> **Date:** 2026-07-29
> **Deciders:** Chief Software Architect, Lead Developer
> **Supersedes:** None

---

## Context

StoryOS is an AI-native platform where AI agents continuously analyze story content, propose knowledge inferences, detect contradictions, and suggest additions to the story's authoritative knowledge state. As AI agent capability grows, a critical governance question arises: under what conditions may AI agents modify Canon — the confirmed, authoritative truth of the story universe?

Canon is not merely data. It is the creator's intellectual record of their story's ground truth. Characters' traits, events' outcomes, world rules, relationship states — all of these reflect the creator's specific, intentional choices about their narrative. An incorrect or unauthorized Canon modification does not create a data inconsistency; it corrupts the creative work itself.

This decision sits at the intersection of AI capability, creator sovereignty, and platform trust. Getting it wrong in either direction has serious consequences: too permissive, and AI silently rewrites creators' stories; too restrictive, and AI delivers no value beyond annotation. The correct balance must be defined architecturally, not left to configuration.

This decision corresponds to **DD-004** in the System Architecture Document (`docs/architecture/architecture.md`) and is enforced by Architectural Principle P6 (Canon Sovereign).

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Option A: Fully Autonomous AI Canon Modification

AI agents are granted direct write access to Canon. When an agent detects a clear inference (e.g., a character was described as arriving in City X in Chapter 3 and this is not recorded in Canon), the agent may write the inferred fact directly to Canon without creator review. Agents operate with confidence thresholds — inferences above a configured threshold are written autonomously; below it, the inference is surfaced for review.

**Pros:**
- Maximum automation — Canon stays synchronized with story content without requiring creator interaction for every fact
- Reduces creator workload for straightforward, high-confidence inferences
- AI agent value is delivered immediately without a review bottleneck
- Reflects real capability of modern large language models for extracting explicit facts from clear prose
- Faster consistency checking response cycles — Canon is updated as content is written rather than waiting for creator review sessions

**Cons:**
- AI inference is probabilistic, not deterministic — even high-confidence extractions have non-zero error rates. At story-universe scale (thousands of facts extracted over months), probabilistic errors accumulate into systematic Canon corruption
- A creator who discovers their story's Canon contains facts they did not intend has no reliable way to know how many other unreviewed AI inferences exist — they must audit the entire Canon
- Confidence thresholds are not semantically meaningful to the creator — a 95% confidence score on an AI inference still means 5% of those inferences are wrong, and the creator cannot know which ones
- Fully autonomous Canon writes violate Architectural Principle P6 (Canon Sovereign) — Canon is the creator's intellectual record, and no automated process may define what the creator's story truth is
- In a collaborative team setting, autonomous AI Canon modification could conflict with in-progress creative decisions that are not yet captured in content — AI cannot know what a writer intends but has not yet written
- Platform liability for corrupted Canon is unacceptable — if an AI autonomously writes incorrect facts into an enterprise customer's IP record, the platform bears responsibility for the creative damage
- Once discovered, autonomous AI Canon errors undermine all creator trust in the platform — "did the AI change other things I didn't notice?" becomes a permanent anxiety

---

### Option B: Semi-Autonomous with Periodic Review Queue

AI agents propose Canon changes as a batch. Instead of requiring creator review of each individual proposal, proposed changes accumulate in a queue and are presented to the creator in periodic sessions (daily, weekly, or when explicitly requested). The creator reviews the batch, approves or rejects proposals in bulk, and the approved set is committed to Canon atomically. For certain low-risk categories of inference (factual extractions with very high confidence, correction of obvious data entry errors), an option exists to enable auto-approval after a configurable delay if the creator does not explicitly reject.

**Pros:**
- Reduces review friction relative to per-proposal confirmation — batch review is more efficient than individual confirmations
- The delay-then-auto-approve option for low-risk categories reduces routine manual work
- Still provides a review mechanism — most AI Canon changes receive creator review
- Practical compromise between full autonomy and full manual control
- Better suited to creators who prefer reviewing changes asynchronously rather than in real time

**Cons:**
- The delay-then-auto-approve mechanism is a pathway for AI to modify Canon without explicit creator confirmation — it violates P6 in practice even if not in principle
- Batch review creates a review backlog problem — if a creator is inactive for several days, hundreds of proposed Canon changes may accumulate, making meaningful review impractical
- Batched proposals create temporal consistency problems — the system operates on unconfirmed proposed Canon for days at a time, and AI agents may reason over a Canon state that has not been creator-validated
- The "low-risk category" definition is a configurable policy — it will inevitably be expanded over time through organizational pressure, progressively eroding the human confirmation requirement
- Enterprise customers with compliance requirements (content provenance, IP audit trails) cannot accept a model where Canon changes may have been auto-approved without explicit creator action
- Creates inconsistent audit trail semantics — some Canon changes are attributed to a creator's explicit action; others are attributed to "timeout auto-approval." The audit record cannot clearly distinguish informed creator intent from passive non-rejection.

---

### Option C: All Canon Modifications Require Explicit Creator Confirmation *(Selected)*

No AI agent, automated process, or system component may write to Canon. All Canon modifications must originate from an explicit, attributable creator action — a deliberate confirmation event in the system. AI agents may propose Canon changes by adding entries to the Proposal Queue marked as `AI_PROPOSED`. These proposals are surfaced to the creator through the platform interface. The creator reviews each proposal individually and either confirms it (promoting it to Canon) or rejects it (dismissing it with an optional note). Only the creator's confirmation event triggers the Canon write. This path is enforced architecturally — AI agents do not have write access to Canon at the layer level.

**Pros:**
- Canon integrity is an absolute guarantee — every fact in Canon is traceable to an explicit creator confirmation, with no ambiguity about authorship or intent
- AI agent errors cannot corrupt Canon silently — proposals that are wrong are rejected and logged, leaving Canon intact
- Full audit trail clarity — every Canon modification event is attributed to a specific human actor's confirmed decision, with a timestamp and the AI proposal that motivated it
- Enterprise IP compliance is satisfied — the creator's Canon is provably their own authorized record, not an AI-modified approximation of it
- Creator trust is structurally protected — the platform can guarantee "AI never changes your Canon without you approving it" without caveats
- Works correctly in collaborative team settings — Canon changes go through the same confirmation flow regardless of whether the proposal was AI-generated or human-originated
- Prevents adversarial exploitation — even a compromised or malfunctioning AI agent cannot propagate changes into Canon because the write path does not exist at the architectural level
- Aligns AI agent value correctly — agents deliver value through analysis, flagging, and well-reasoned proposals, not through autonomous modification

**Cons:**
- Adds a confirmation step between AI inference and Canon incorporation — creators who want rapid, unreviewed AI extraction must adapt to the review workflow
- High-volume AI inference scenarios (importing a large manuscript with thousands of entity references) generate large proposal queues that require creator time to process
- Platform perceived as "less autonomous" than a fully AI-driven tool — may be a disadvantage in marketing against competitors who offer fully autonomous AI
- If creators habitually rubber-stamp proposals without meaningful review (clicking "Confirm All"), the human confirmation step becomes ceremonial rather than substantive — though this is a creator behavior issue, not a platform defect
- Initial AI agent value delivery is slower — AI cannot immediately enrich Canon from imported content; it must wait for creator review cycles

---

## Decision

All Canon modifications — regardless of source, confidence level, or content type — require an explicit creator confirmation event. AI agents write only to the Proposal Queue with `AI_PROPOSED` status. The Canon write path is not accessible to any AI agent, automated process, or system component. Creator confirmation is the sole mechanism by which `AI_PROPOSED` knowledge transitions to `CANON` status.

---

## Rationale

The principle underlying this decision is creator sovereignty. Canon is not a database of inferred facts — it is the creator's authoritative record of their story's truth. The moment the platform allows any automated process to modify that record without the creator's explicit consent, the creator is no longer the author of their own Canon. They are a reviewer of an AI-generated approximation, hoping it captures their intent correctly.

This matters more in the creative domain than in almost any other domain. In a billing system, an AI that auto-corrects an address with high confidence is acceptable — the address is a fact that exists independent of the system's record. In a story universe, a "fact" about a character's backstory is not external truth awaiting discovery — it is a creative decision that belongs to the creator. The AI has no basis to determine that its inference reflects creator intent, regardless of how confident its scoring is.

Option A optimizes for automation at the cost of creator sovereignty, platform trust, and Canon integrity. Option B introduces complexity and ambiguity through the auto-approval mechanism and batch review model without resolving the fundamental problem — Canon can still be modified without explicit creator action.

Option C accepts the friction of a confirmation step in exchange for an absolute guarantee that Canon reflects the creator's choices. This is not excessive friction — it is the correct trust model for an enterprise platform managing intellectual property. The AI's role is to make the right answer easier to confirm, not to decide the right answer on the creator's behalf.

---

## Consequences

### Positive
- Canon integrity is an absolute guarantee — every Canon entry is traceable to an explicit, attributable, time-stamped creator decision
- Platform can make a legally and compliance-valid claim: "No AI agent modifies Canon without creator approval" — no caveats, no thresholds, no exceptions
- AI agent errors are contained entirely within the Proposal Queue — they cannot propagate into Canon
- Audit trail is unambiguous — Canon write events are always creator actions, never system events
- Creator trust is structurally enforced — creators do not need to trust the platform's policy configuration to trust their Canon is theirs
- The Proposal Queue provides a complete record of AI reasoning and proposals, independent of whether they were accepted — valuable for understanding AI agent behavior over time
- Third-party AI agent plugins are automatically subject to the same constraint — they have no Canon write access regardless of their capability declarations

### Negative / Trade-offs
- AI agent value delivery is gated on creator review cycles — Canon does not update autonomously, requiring creator engagement to be timely
- Large manuscript imports generate large proposal queues — creators importing substantial content face significant review workloads before Canon reflects that content
- The platform may be perceived as requiring more manual work than competitors offering fully autonomous AI Canon management
- Proposal queue management becomes a UX design challenge at scale — surfacing, prioritizing, grouping, and batch-reviewing proposals effectively requires significant interface investment
- If a creator is unresponsive, the Proposal Queue grows without bound and AI agents may reason over an increasingly stale Canon — queue expiry and priority management policies must be designed carefully
- Ceremonial confirmation (approving proposals without meaningful review) is a behavioral risk that the platform cannot structurally prevent — it can only be mitigated through UX design that encourages deliberate review

## Compliance

Future developers verify this decision is being respected by confirming the following:

1. **No AI agent has Canon write access**: At the AI Layer boundary, no agent role includes Canon write permissions. The Security Module's permission matrix must explicitly exclude `CANON_WRITE` from all AI agent role definitions.
2. **`AI_PROPOSED` status is immutable by AI agents**: AI agents may create records with `AI_PROPOSED` status in the Proposal Queue. They may not change a record's status from `AI_PROPOSED` to any other status — including `CANON`. Status promotion is exclusively a creator action.
3. **Canon writes are attributed to a creator confirmation event**: Every Canon modification record in the Audit System must reference a creator confirmation event ID. Canon writes without a linked human confirmation event must be treated as integrity violations and trigger an immediate audit alert.
4. **No auto-approval mechanism may be implemented**: No feature, configuration setting, timer, or system process may automatically promote `AI_PROPOSED` knowledge to `CANON` status without a real-time creator interaction. Any such feature proposal must be escalated to the Chief Software Architect and rejected against this ADR.
5. **Architectural review gate**: Any module that proposes a new AI agent capability must explicitly document how that capability routes through the Proposal Queue and creator confirmation flow. Capabilities that would require Canon write access must be rejected at the design review stage.
